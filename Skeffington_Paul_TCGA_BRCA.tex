\section*{Introduction}
Breast cancer represents a major global public health dilemma requiring multinational synergy in research, and pharmaceutical development. In 2024, approximately 2.4 million women were diagnosed with breast cancer world-wide, with an estimated 694,000 associated deaths\parencite{who2026breast}. Despite advances in screening, and treatment, breast cancer remains a biologically heterogeneous disease, with considerable variable in molecular characteristic and clinical behavior.

Through the contribution of scientists globally, gene-expression research has identified distinct molecular subtypes, Luminal A, Luminal B, Her-2 enriched, and Basal-like breast cancers \parencite{parker2009}. The classifications demonstrate differences in tumor biology and give researcher a frame work for investigating variance in gene expressions. The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), has established an extensive molecular database for investigating breast cancer heterogeneity \parencite{tcga2012}. 

Selected genes, ESR1, and ERBB2 were selected for their association with estrogen signaling and HER2- related signaling \parencite{tcga2012} ESR1 (Estrogen Receptor 1) encodes estrogen receptor alpha, which regulates hormone-dependent gene expression and is strongly associated with luminal breast cancer subtypes \parencite{parker2009}. EBBR2 (HER2) encodes a receptor tyrosine kinase involved in celluar growth and survival, with amplification or overespression assicated with HER2- positive breast cancer \parencit{tcga2012}.
